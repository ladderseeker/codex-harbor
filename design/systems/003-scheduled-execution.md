## Schedules and occurrence authority

Create one-time and recurring schedules with prompt, project, workspace mode, model/effort, permitted profile, timezone, next-run preview, overlap policy, and missed-run policy. Provide pause/resume, run-now, cancel-current-run, occurrence history, and an attention inbox. Scheduled work uses the same durable conversation/approval machinery as manual work.

Default a standalone occurrence to a new conversation and worktree. Reusing an existing conversation is explicit and respects its active turn. A paused schedule stops future admission, not an already-running occurrence; cancellation is separate. Email/Slack/GitHub event triggers and outbound notifications are excluded. Authenticated domain API routes are included; Explicit PAT scopes also protect programmatic access.

Persist schedule revision/owner, timezone/rule, next due time, effective policy, and occurrence identity. Create occurrence and its internal phase/job intent transactionally. A recurring occurrence key combines schedule ID, rule revision, and intended local minute, with its resolved UTC instant stored alongside; configuration revision or grant renewal does not redefine that identity. The queue may retry safe internal work, but ambiguous Codex delivery requires P007 reconciliation.

Choose and expose timezone/DST semantics explicitly: skip nonexistent local times and run once at the first matching instant for an ambiguous repeated local time with no configurable second-fold alternative. Default to recording missed occurrences without unbounded catch-up; default overlapping occurrences to a visible skipped result. Clock adjustments and restarts must not duplicate occurrence identity.

Revalidate current project access, model support, resource limits, and permission ceiling at execution. Unattended work never treats absence as approval. Approval deadlines have explicit wait/deny/interruption behavior. Schedules with revoked authority, removed projects, unavailable runtimes, or unknown recovery state fail or wait visibly. Protect all management/history endpoints and rendered prompts/results.

Scheduling uses shared workspace/conversation admission and explicit token route scopes; it does not depend on a file editor, terminal or deployment UI. Installed module handling follows the managed restoration contract. There is no upstream Codex scheduling method assumed by this implementation.

### Time rules and bounded evaluation

- Support one-time local date/time at minute precision and a strict five-field cron expression: minute, hour, day of month, month and day of week. Accept numeric values, `*`, comma lists, inclusive ranges and positive steps; reject seconds/year fields, names, macros, `?`, `L`, `W`, `#`, randomization and embedded commands. Sunday is `0` or `7`, normalized to `0`. A day field is restricted when its source contains no `*`, including an explicit full-domain range; wildcard steps retain wildcard semantics. When both day fields are restricted, their match is OR; otherwise both expanded fields must match. This follows the [documented five-field restriction rule](https://github.com/cronie-crond/cronie/blob/master/man/crontab.5), with Harbor’s separately specified first-fold DST policy. Bound the expression to 200 bytes and each expanded field to its finite domain. Reject invalid dates and rules with no future occurrence within five calendar years.
- Require a server-validated IANA timezone, with `UTC` supported explicitly; display its canonical identifier. The UI submits a local minute and timezone for one-time work and shows the resolved UTC instant before activation. Reject a nonexistent one-time local minute or a one-time instant already past. Recurrence skips nonexistent local minutes and uses the earlier instant of a repeated local minute. These are the only supported DST policies initially, visibly stated in preview; there is no silent shift of `02:30` to `03:30`.
- Pin `@js-temporal/polyfill` 0.5.1 alongside Node 24.11.1 for local-calendar conversion. Enumerate matching calendar dates and finite hour/minute fields, map each local minute using `disambiguation: "earlier"`, and require a roundtrip to the same local date/time before accepting it. This roundtrip rejects gaps, including transitions wider than one hour. Do not assume that selecting `earlier` itself rejects a gap. Sort accepted instants, discard instants at/before the persisted cursor, and never emit the later fold. [Pinned package](https://github.com/js-temporal/temporal-polyfill/tree/v0.5.1), [Temporal disambiguation semantics](https://tc39.es/proposal-temporal/docs/zoneddatetime.html#static-methods).
- One bounded evaluator supplies API previews and supervisor decisions. Preview returns at most eight instants within five calendar years, including local time, UTC offset and UTC time; fewer than eight is valid when the horizon is exhausted. Use calendar-field jumps rather than a minute-by-minute five-year scan, with at most 2,000 inspected calendar dates, 4,096 timezone conversions and a two-second computation deadline per call. An exhausted work budget is an explicit evaluation error, never an invented next time. Yield CPU work in bounded batches so a malformed or sparse rule cannot stall approvals or manual turns.
- Persist a distinct rule revision, timezone, local occurrence key, UTC intended instant, scheduler cursor and Node/ICU/timezone-data/polyfill fingerprint. The recurring identity is unique for `(schedule ID, rule revision, intended local minute)` and additionally stores the selected UTC instant. Metadata edits and pause/resume do not manufacture another rule revision. Material rule/timezone edits start a new future cursor at the committed edit time and never revisit earlier slots. A timezone-data fingerprint change pauses affected schedules for explicit preview/renewal; it does not reinterpret accepted occurrences.
- Read due time from the database clock. The private deterministic fixture may substitute a run-owned database clock row under fixture configuration, which production rejects; auth expiry, leases and approval deadlines still use their real security clocks. Persisted cursor/high-water marks never move backward after a wall-clock rollback. A forward jump follows the same missed-run policy as downtime. Display the last observed scheduler time and any clock/revision pause reason without claiming an exact host-clock diagnosis.

### Unattended authority

- Activating a schedule creates an explicit, revocable, bounded execution grant for that schedule revision: owner pin, instance ID, project, workspace mode/target, prompt hash, model/effort, permission profile, policy ceiling reference and grant epoch. Browser-created grants last 30 days by default, configurable from one to 90 days. Show expiry and allow deliberate renewal. They are independent of the creator's browser cookie, so browser closure, logout and ordinary session expiry do not break unattended work.
- A PAT can activate, renew or materially edit a schedule only with new `schedules:manage` plus existing `execute`, a grant for its project, and a sufficient permission ceiling. Record the source PAT ID; the schedule grant expires no later than that PAT and every new preparation/dispatch checks its current expiry, revocation, owner/instance binding, scopes and project grant. Rebinding a PAT-created schedule to browser authority requires a fresh explicit browser activation. Existing tokens receive no new scope automatically. `schedules:read` protects list/detail/preview/history; management routes require `schedules:manage`; cancelling an occurrence additionally requires `cancel`. Cancel and pause do not require the target run's write ceiling.
- Creating a worktree/copy is a P003 storage mutation even if the resulting Codex turn is read-only: the administrator and source PAT ceilings must permit `workspace-write` for standalone preparation. Offer existing-conversation mode when only read-only execution is permitted. Extract the existing P003 admission into a shared domain service with an explicit narrow internal-grant path; do not remove the browser-only gate from its public workspace-management routes or duplicate their quota, identity, receipt and reservation checks. Once that service commits an exact accepted storage command, its original fixed-effect grant permits receipt reconciliation after expiry/pause, but never admits the following Codex turn without fresh schedule authority.
- Use a distinct internal schedule-grant actor understood by the shared authorization function, never an HTTP credential format or browser-supplied actor. It grants only the recorded P003 preparation and exact P001 turn intent, and cannot answer approvals, create another schedule, issue credentials, alter policy, or select a different project/prompt. Public requests cannot provide this actor identity. Keep ordinary browser/PAT authority checks unchanged.
- Acquire the owner-identity gate, source actor where applicable, schedule grant, project, workspace, conversation and occurrence locks in one documented order. Recheck fresh authority after relevant waits and immediately before an external helper or Codex send. Pausing/revoking a grant prevents not-yet-dispatched work; already dispatched preparation may only reconcile its exact accepted effect, and an already running turn retains its accepted execution grant. Renewal or editing never upgrades a queued occurrence's captured authority. Approval answers always require a currently authorized human/API actor through the existing approval path.
- Emergency stop pauses all schedules and prevents further preparation/admission. Removing or archiving a project, changing its ceiling below the grant, grant expiry/revocation, unavailable model/account, exhausted storage/history capacity and unresolved writer ownership produce visible attention states. None enables fallback models, broader profiles, unapproved roots or automatic uncertainty acknowledgement. Recovery/renewal does not itself replay the blocked occurrence.

### Occurrence admission and external effects

- Store schedule configuration, current grant and immutable accepted occurrence snapshots in Harbor tables. Separate configuration revision, rule revision and grant epoch. A recurring occurrence has stable occurrence/workspace/session/turn IDs before external work; run-now instead has a separate unique request identity and never consumes or moves a recurring cursor. Use normal actor/route idempotency and revision CAS for create/edit/activate/pause/run-now/cancel. A changed payload with an old key conflicts, including after a dropped response.
- Create the occurrence, next cursor and its pg-boss job in the same PostgreSQL transaction, using pg-boss 12.5.2's transaction connection adapter. Queue payloads contain opaque occurrence IDs and epochs only, never prompts, secrets, paths or executable code. The job wakes a bounded state reconciler; Harbor occurrence/operation rows remain authoritative. Duplicate delivery and worker restart are expected, and job completion is not a completed Codex result. [Pinned pg-boss job/transaction API](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/12.5.2/docs/api/jobs.md).
- Persist phases such as `accepted`, `preparing_workspace`, `queued_turn`, `running`, `attention`, and final `succeeded`, `failed`, `cancelled`, `skipped` or `uncertain`. Keep the linked P001 operation's actual outcome and P007 acknowledgement separately; an attention flag is not a replacement for execution state. Dispatching Codex without an acknowledged result is uncertain, never a retryable queue failure. Restart safely resumes only an undispatched internal phase or the exact P003 receipt reconciliation; it never repeats an ambiguous native turn or creates a replacement workspace for an ambiguous allocation.
- Standalone Git occurrences default to a new P003 worktree and new conversation. The base policy is the registered source's `HEAD` at occurrence admission or an explicitly fixed commit OID. Resolve `HEAD` through the workspace service’s trusted inspection while holding its source/project reservation, persist the resulting immutable OID, and use the same OID on every safe retry; dirty files are excluded as in P003. No arbitrary ref syntax or Git flags enter the scheduling API. Non-Git projects visibly use a new P003 copy with the existing bounded snapshot/durability contract. The accepted copy operation owns its source reservation and receipt. Every resulting checkout and native home belongs to the project's quota unit. Keep the artifact/result links even when a turn fails.
- Reusing a conversation is an explicit alternative pinned to its project/workspace. Its configured model/effort/profile must be valid for that conversation's next turn. At each occurrence, an active/queued manual turn, uncertain session, terminal/file writer or project metadata reservation follows the visible busy policy; do not insert automation ahead of manual work or acquire another feature's writer. New conversations and existing ones both use the workspace service’s shared admission service and the conversation service’s ordinary operation limits. File/terminal operations use the same typed reservation; scheduling has no alternative lock.
- At most one nonfinal occurrence per schedule may prepare or run. Overlap policy is `skip` initially; record which occurrence or workspace condition prevented admission. A runtime exit with unretired background work keeps the occurrence/writer busy until existing confirmed-retirement semantics settle it. Never remove or reset a generated worktree automatically: the owner reviews/archives/removes it through P003. Reaching the workspace service’s 16-workspace limit pauses new preparation with a useful link to workspace management.

### Missed work, controls and attention

- A due slot observed within 60 seconds of its intended instant is eligible for normal admission. The default missed policy skips older slots. Optional `catch_up` admits at most three missed slots from the latest 24 hours, oldest first, and only one at a time; overlaps are still skipped. Freeze the missed-window decision and chosen identities transactionally so worker retries cannot continually refill the catch-up batch. A separately due current slot must not cause a second simultaneous occurrence.
- Cap an individual scan to 128 past occurrence details; represent a longer skipped interval by one bounded range record with rule revision, exclusive/inclusive cursor bounds, reason, and an exact count only when it was actually computed. Label an uncounted range as such. Advance the cursor past the whole recorded interval atomically; do not enqueue a row per missed minute or silently lose the gap. Paused time is recorded as paused, not eligible catch-up. Resume starts future admission from its committed time; editing also leaves the prior revision's missed/accepted history intact.
- Pause stops future and accepted-but-undispatched work without cancelling an active turn. Run-now is allowed for a paused schedule only through a deliberate current execute authorization and its own one-shot grant; it leaves the schedule paused. Reject run-now while another occurrence is nonfinal. Cancel-current-run targets the exact occurrence/operation: cancel an undispatched phase without starting it, or use the existing cancellation/confirmed-retirement machinery for running work. Cancellation never undoes completed filesystem effects, deletes its checkout, or turns uncertainty into success.
- Offline approval/input uses the conversation service’s existing five-minute deadline and fail-closed denial/interruption behavior. Show pending requests and final expiry in a schedule attention inbox linked to the actual conversation. A schedule grant never supplies approval or user-input answers. An owner may inspect/recover an uncertain occurrence through P007; a deliberate new continuation is a separately linked human operation and does not change the original occurrence into a successful scheduled run. Further recurring occurrences remain paused while unacknowledged uncertainty or unresolved preparation remains.
- After a visible resource, policy or authority failure, pause repeated automatic admission and preserve the original reason. Resume/renew checks current prerequisites and previews the next future slots; it does not silently retry failed, missed or uncertain occurrences. The owner can explicitly run now as a new intent after addressing the cause. The public API has no arbitrary retry/replay switch.

### API, UI and storage bounds

- Publish versioned schemas for `/api/v1/schedules`, `/schedules/preview`, `/schedules/{id}`, `/schedules/{id}/activation`, `/schedules/{id}/pause`, `/schedules/{id}/runs`, `/schedules/{id}/runs/{occurrenceId}/cancel`, and `/schedule-attention`. GET routes are read-only; mutations use authenticated commands, exact browser Origin/CSRF where applicable, current project authorization and the existing idempotency contract. Unknown routes remain browser-only under the token policy’s fail-closed registry. Return redacted public DTOs, excluding source credential identifiers, grant actor strings, internal paths and queue details.
- Add a responsive Schedules area, project filter, create/edit form, next-eight preview, timezone/DST and authority-expiry explanation, enabled/paused/attention indicators, explicit run-now/cancel controls, and paginated occurrence history linked to workspace/conversation/result. Display intended time separately from admission/start/end time and distinguish skipped, cancelled, failed, uncertain and awaiting approval. Use plain text for prompt previews/errors and the existing safe conversation rendering for results. Preserve an unsaved form draft in memory on a revision conflict; never replace another tab's changes silently.
- Limit prompts to 32 KiB, titles to 160 UTF-8 bytes, 16 enabled schedules per project/64 per instance, and 64 retained schedules per project/256 per instance. Bound accepted occurrence metadata to 4 KiB excluding its one bounded prompt snapshot; retain at most 256 settled occurrence/range records per schedule and 8,192 per instance for 90 days, pruning the oldest settled rows first. Preserve all nonfinal/unacknowledged-uncertain ownership and reserve one cancellation plus one acknowledgement reference per retained occurrence. At most one unresolved occurrence per schedule and 256 per instance may remain; reaching that ceiling stops new admission with attention rather than evicting evidence. Only after the recovery service’s explicit acknowledgement and confirmed retirement of the original owner may an uncertain occurrence stop consuming unresolved ownership. Its original uncertain outcome and acknowledgement remain immutable history, governed by the same finite settled occurrence retention rather than a new exemption; the conversation's original operation/history follows P001/P007 retention independently. This does not release a successor writer or erase the cursor. A small permanent cursor/last-pruned summary prevents pruned recurring identities from being created again.
- Retain at most 32 configuration/grant revisions per schedule, never deleting a revision referenced by unresolved work. At that ceiling reject new ordinary revisions while keeping pause/cancel available. Management idempotency uses the existing finite retry window with at most 128 retained command results per schedule, reserving pause and per-occurrence cancellation slots; failed retries reuse records. Run-now is additionally limited to ten accepted intents per schedule per hour. These limits are enforced transactionally before enqueueing or copying data; returning a throttled error must not allocate an orphan operation.
- List/history/attention pages contain at most 50 rows, with opaque filter-bound keyset cursors, maximum five-minute cursor age, and bounded text snippets. The supervisor reconciles at most 16 schedules and four preparation/admission operations per tick with a finite queue; no timer per occurrence or unbounded promise fan-out. The history view reports retention/pruning and aggregate missed ranges honestly. P001 session/event and P003 physical storage limits remain independently enforced.

### Backup, recovery and verification integration

- Register all schedule, grant/revision, occurrence/range, command-result and cursor tables with the managed deployment service’s installed module registry. Generated workspaces/native homes remain in their existing complete project units; no separate hidden scheduled-work tree is introduced. Include the time-engine fingerprint and original intended instants in recovery metadata.
- D006 drain freezes schedule configuration and preparation with other mutations. Backup refuses unresolved external preparation unless its owning receipt/retirement contract permits a coherent historical checkpoint. Restore revokes every schedule/one-shot grant, pauses every schedule, and makes old queue jobs nondispatchable. Preserve completed and uncertain occurrences with source provenance, invalidate source-host recovery readiness, and require explicit new destination activation. No restore worker contacts source-host runners, catches up automatically, or treats a new browser login as schedule renewal.
- Exercise the actual browser/API/PostgreSQL/pg-boss/supervisor path with no browser open at due time. Fixtures may replace Codex/OIDC boundaries and provide the private scheduler clock; they may not replace Harbor scheduling, authority, workspace or persistence. Add distinct adversarial time/parser checks for spring gaps, first folds, non-hour and whole-day timezone transitions, leap years, invalid/sparse rules, backward/forward clock jumps, fingerprint changes and bounded dense catch-up.
- Crash at transaction/job commit, stable-ID creation, workspace allocation receipt, pre-wire authorization, native send and acknowledgement; verify exact IDs/effects, explicit uncertainty and current authority after lock waits. Cover revoked/expired source PATs, browser logout with continuing valid grants, epoch renewal races, pause versus admission, manual/other-feature writers, dropped control responses, approval expiry and every capacity's reserved controls. Managed deployment acceptance includes restored paused schedules and blocked stale jobs. Changed adapter/launcher boundaries require their pinned-runtime/Linux lanes; unchanged inherited boundaries need exact source-delta evidence. P008-07 remains mandatory and blocked without dedicated live credentials.

## Installed schema and restoration

Previously applied migration bytes are immutable. The fixed code-owned installed registry includes the six schedule tables; generated workspace and native content remain in the existing project inventory. Status distinguishes retained queued occurrences from active native or storage effects. Maintenance freezes new schedule admission and preparation; active effects must settle under their existing receipt and retirement rules before drain succeeds. Administrator interruption also pauses schedules and revokes all grants.

Fresh-instance rebind preserves original schedule and occurrence records in the historical deployment inventory, pauses every schedule, revokes regular and one-shot grants, invalidates source command authority and removes the private fixture clock. Undispatched occurrences become cancelled; a linked turn already classified uncertain stays uncertain. Existing completed and uncertain outcomes remain historical. Only explicit destination activation under current authority can issue a fresh grant; stale queue notifications cannot renew one.

Acceptance includes a fresh full schema and a real PostgreSQL upgrade from the exact predecessor migration map containing 016 to the unchanged map plus 013. Lower-ID compatibility remains conditional on the exact supported predecessor artifact and migration map; no migration renumbering or generic bypass is introduced. Local metadata rebind and compatibility evidence do not claim protected off-host restore or promotion.
